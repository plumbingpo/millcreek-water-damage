Attic Water Damage: What Millcreek Homeowners Should Know

Water reaches an attic from two directions. Rain gets in from above through worn shingles, failed flashing, or the seals around vents and chimneys. The house itself sends moisture upward too, and in a poorly ventilated attic it condenses on cold sheathing. In both cases, almost nobody climbs up to check. For Millcreek homeowners, our crew examines the attic, identifies where the water comes from, and dries the space.

On the typical attic job, soaked insulation is the main issue. Once wet, both cellulose and fiberglass lose R-value, and heating and cooling the home costs more. Even worse, it keeps water pressed against the framing and the ceiling drywall for weeks. Saturated material comes out, air movers and dehumidifiers dry the wood, and any mold on the sheathing or rafters gets treated.

Warning Signs

  • Streaks or dark staining on the roof deck's underside and on the rafters
  • Closets on the top floor, or the attic, that smell musty
  • After rain, a stain or brown ring on a top floor ceiling that looks worse
  • Rusty nail tips poking through the sheathing, sometimes with water beads on them
  • Insulation that looks matted, flattened, or darker than the insulation around it
  • Attic ducts and metal hangers showing drips, rust or water marks
What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Air Conditioner Drips

Air handlers, with their drain pans and condensate lines, sit in many attics. When the pan overflows or the drain clogs, insulation gets wet for weeks without any rain as a warning.

Lost Insulation Value

Compressed, damp insulation does very little insulating, and it may not recover after it dries. Rooms under the wet section run hotter or colder, and energy bills rise.

Water Near Electrical

Cable runs, junction boxes and recessed light housings are all found in attics. Do not enter the attic before someone checks the power, because wet electrical parts can shock you or start a fire.

Bath Fans Venting Inside

Every day, a fan duct that stops short of the roof releases humid bathroom air into the attic. The condensation it leaves on the sheathing can pass for a roof leak.

How We Fix It

Our Attic Water Damage Process

Inspect and Trace

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. Thermal imaging and moisture meters show how far it reached.

How the Damage Progresses

The first storm

Insulation Takes It

Rain that makes it through the roof falls into the insulation and is absorbed. Because the rooms below show nothing, the leak is missed and no roof repair happens.

24 to 48 hours

Mold May Begin

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

A few weeks on

Ceiling Stains Appear

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. The drywall may sag, and rings of brown staining appear on the ceiling of the room below.

Months later

Wood Starts Rotting

Sheathing delaminates and loses its hold on the roofing nails. Large areas of roof decking are covered in mold, while rafters that never dry begin to decay.
