What's Typically Covered

Sudden and accidental water damage is covered under the majority of standard homeowner's policies. This includes burst pipes, appliance failures (washing machines, water heaters, dishwashers), ice dam damage, accidental overflow from sinks or bathtubs, and sudden HVAC system failures. In those cases a policy usually pays for extraction, drying the structure, replacing damaged materials, and other restoration work, plus temporary living costs if the home is unlivable.

What's Typically NOT Covered

The usual exclusions are sewage backup unless you carry a specific endorsement, outside flooding unless you hold separate flood insurance, groundwater seeping through the foundation, slow leaks and other gradual damage, and anything traced to neglected or deferred maintenance. Understanding these exclusions before a loss occurs lets you add endorsements or separate policies to fill coverage gaps.

How to File Your Claim the Right Way

When water damage occurs, take these steps to protect your claim, starting with photos and video of everything before cleanup begins. Report the loss to your insurer as soon as you can. Mitigation matters too, meaning sensible action to stop further damage. Keep damaged items until the adjuster has inspected them. Get professional restoration from an IICRC-certified company. Keep all receipts related to the loss, including temporary repairs and living expenses. Be present during the adjuster's inspection and point out all damage.
