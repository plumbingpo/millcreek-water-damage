Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in Millcreek

Cooling a house also dries the air, which means the unit is constantly making water it must get rid of. Normally it drips off the coil into a pan and leaves through a small drain line. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. We find how far the moisture spread, dry the structure and repair what was damaged. Clearing the drain or repairing the unit remains a job for an HVAC technician, and our schedule can fit around theirs.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut the System DownSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. Flip the air handler's breaker too when water is close to its wiring.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanIf you can reach the pan safely, soak up or wet-vac the standing water so it stops spilling over. Skip this step if it means crossing a soaked attic.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Bring In Two ProsCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.
Our Process

Our AC Leak Water Damage Process

1

Measure the Spread

We use moisture meters and thermal imaging around the air handler, the ceiling or walls next to it, and the flooring outside the closet to find every wet material, not only the stained ones.

2

Remove Water and Wet Drywall

Standing water in the pan, the platform and the carpet is extracted. Saturated or moldy insulation and drywall are removed, which opens the cavity to dry air.

3

Set Drying Equipment

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, usually 3 to 5, while we track the readings. Access to the air handler is kept open for your HVAC technician.

4

Sanitize and Rebuild

Days-old condensate is not clean water, so everything it reached is treated with an antimicrobial. New flooring, baseboard, drywall and insulation follow, and fresh paint makes the area match.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

With AC leaks, insurers mostly look at how long the water was running. If the pan overflowed suddenly and you acted when you found it, many homeowners policies pay for the resulting water damage. Denial is possible when the adjuster concludes the leak ran for weeks or months, since that reads as a gradual or maintenance problem. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Read your policy, and ask us for photos and moisture readings to support the claim.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Missing insulation on cold lines and ductwork, so condensation forms and falls.
  • Coil icing brought on by a filter that was not changed or a refrigerant charge that is low.
  • A condensate pump that burned out or lost power and stopped moving water.
  • Algae, slime and dust from the coil plugging the condensate drain.
  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Closet Walls and Platform

Closet units often sit on a wood platform over the return air box. Leaks decay the stand and climb the closet drywall, concealed by the equipment itself.

Ceilings Under the Unit

When the air handler is overhead, the ceiling under it takes the water. Staining is the early sign, sagging follows, and drywall left wet for days can come down into the room below.

Mold Around the Air Handler

Within 24 to 48 hours mold can start on wet wood and drywall next to the unit, and the return may draw spores into the ducts and around the home.

Floors Outside the Closet

Carpet pad, laminate and hardwood near the closet all get wet from underneath as the leak spreads past the door. Hardwood cups when it is not dried, while laminate swells for good.
